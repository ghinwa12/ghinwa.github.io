Case study 1

DocLab: a healthcare booking platform

Patient mobile app and doctor dashboard, H2Marketplace, 2024

Two applications share one PostgreSQL database with row-level security. Patients find approved doctors and book a slot in a mobile app. Doctors manage clinics, appointments, and patients in a web dashboard.

My role
Built both applications and managed the team that delivered them.
Patient app
Discover approved doctors, filter by specialty and location, book a slot, view the medical file by number, and message a doctor.
Doctor dashboard
Manage clinics and open slots, confirm or decline visits, access patient charts with permission, and track appointments.
Security design
  • Row-level security restricts data by user role and ownership.
  • Role-based access separates patients, doctors, and reviewers.
  • Database functions handle sensitive actions such as booking and unlocking a file.
  • Temporary access grants unlock medical files for 12 hours only.
  • Documents sit in private storage and are served through signed URLs.

Screens show test data.

How the system fits together

Patient mobile appAuthenticated requests through a secure API
Authentication layerSign-up and login, with roles for patient, doctor, and reviewer
Doctor dashboardRole-based access under row-level security
PostgreSQL databaseAppointments, medical records, users and roles, messages and notifications. Row-level security applies to all of it.

Project management, full-stack delivery, database security, role-based access control, team coordination.

Case study 2

Jaye Delivery app: driver bidding failure

Backend validation and incident coordination

The incident
Eligible drivers could not submit bids on available delivery requests. The disruption lasted about 4 hours and affected around 15 to 20 drivers.
Root cause
The backend validation compared a driver’s active delivery count against an outdated limit, so it rejected bids from drivers who were eligible.
My role
  • Worked with the backend developer to trace the validation logic.
  • Confirmed the intended business rules with the product owner.
  • Reviewed the correction against the expected eligibility conditions.
  • Tested the updated validation to confirm that eligible bids were accepted.
Outcome
The condition was corrected, eligible drivers could bid again, and the delivery assignment workflow was restored.

Incident management, business logic analysis, backend coordination, requirements validation, functional testing.
